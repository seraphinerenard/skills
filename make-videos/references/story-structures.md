# Story structures and pacing

Both modes share these structures. The storyboard (one line per scene: claim, visual, duration) is written and approved before any code, and for GTM videos the ideation skill's story phase runs first.

## The GTM arc

Five beats. Total runtime 45 to 90 seconds; past 90 seconds completion rates fall off and the video should become two videos.

| Beat | Job | Duration | Scene content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Earn the next 10 seconds | 2 to 4 s | One claim or one number, full screen. No logo, no intro. |
| Problem | Make the pain concrete | 8 to 15 s | The cost of the status quo in numbers the viewer recognizes. |
| Turn | Show the product doing the thing | 10 to 25 s | Real interface in a ScreenFrame, one workflow, no feature tour. |
| Proof | Make the claim believable | 8 to 15 s | StatCounters with baselines, a named customer result, or a live metric. |
| CTA | Say what to do next | 4 to 8 s | LogoReveal plus one action: a URL, a date, a booking link. One action, not three. |

Rules that make the arc work:

- **One claim per scene.** If a scene needs two sentences on screen, it is two scenes.
- **The hook is a claim, never a logo.** Logos open videos nobody chose to watch; claims open videos people share.
- **The product appears by second 15.** GTM videos that delay the product read as ads and get skipped.
- **Proof numbers carry baselines.** "4-hour close" means nothing; "closes in 4 hours, down from 11" is proof. The StatCounter component makes the baseline a required prop for this reason.

## The instructional arc

Four beats. Total runtime scales with step count; 60 to 180 seconds is the working range, and past that the video becomes a chaptered series.

| Beat | Job | Duration | Scene content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective | Say what the viewer will be able to do | 4 to 8 s | "By the end you can X" as one sentence, plus any prerequisite. |
| Setup | Show the starting state | 5 to 10 s | Where to be, what to have open. One screen, labelled. |
| Steps | Do the thing | 10 to 20 s each | One step per scene: action, then visible result. Number the steps on screen. |
| Recap | Fix it in memory | 6 to 10 s | The steps as a single list, plus where to go when it breaks. |

Rules:

- **Show the result of every step before the next step starts.** The viewer is checking their own screen against yours; give them the checkpoint.
- **Never scroll and talk at the same time.** Movement and new information compete; do one, then the other.
- **State the failure mode.** The most replayed moment in any tutorial is "if you see this error instead". Script it deliberately.

## Pacing tables

Voice-over density:

| Measure | Budget |
|---|---|
| VO pace | at most 2.5 words per second (150 words per minute) |
| VO script for a 60 s video | at most 150 words |
| Silence | at least 15% of runtime carries no VO; numbers need quiet to land |

On-screen text:

| Measure | Budget |
|---|---|
| Words on screen at once | at most 12 outside of UI screenshots |
| Hold time | reading time × 1.5, minimum 2 s (reading time: 3 words per second) |
| Title size | readable at phone size: 5% of frame height minimum for body, 8% for titles |

Shot length:

| Scene type | Length |
|---|---|
| Claim or stat card | 2 to 5 s |
| Product footage | 4 to 8 s per workflow moment |
| Any single static frame | 6 s maximum before something must move or cut |

## Scene-count arithmetic

Storyboards are budgeted before writing: a 60-second GTM video at the durations above is 7 to 10 scenes. If the storyboard lands at 15 scenes, the story has too many claims; cut claims, not seconds per scene. Compressing every scene to hit a runtime produces the fast-flicker video that nobody retains.
