FINDINGS REPORT: UNCONSTRAINING / DEMAND RECOVERY FOR STOCKOUT-CENSORED DEMAND IN RETAIL/CPG SKU-LEVEL FORECASTING
All URLs accessed 2026-07-12.

=== 1. THE CORE PROBLEM AND TERMINOLOGY ===

Observed sales are a right-censored observation of demand: when an item stocks out, sales record only the inventory that was available, so demand above the stock level is unobserved. Training a forecaster on raw sales therefore biases forecasts downward, which lowers future orders and service levels and can trigger a self-reinforcing "spiral-down". Trapero, de Frutos & Pedregal, "Demand forecasting under lost sales stock policies", International Journal of Forecasting 40(3), 2024, pp. 1055-1068, state that in the presence of stockouts sales underestimate demand, forecasts are biased downward, and the induced under-ordering "may cause a spiral-down effect" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207023000961, accessed 2026-07-12; RePEc record with volume/pages: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v40y2024i3p1055-1068.html, accessed 2026-07-12).

The spiral-down effect was formalized in revenue management by Cooper, Homem-de-Mello & Kleywegt, "Models of the Spiral-Down Effect in Revenue Management", Operations Research 54(5), 2006, pp. 968-987: protection levels set from censored high-fare sales cause high-fare sales, protection levels, and revenues to decrease monotonically over repeated re-estimation (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v54y2006i5p968-987.html, accessed 2026-07-12; PDF: https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/8/1346/files/2020/09/spiral.pdf, accessed 2026-07-12). The paper won the 2007 INFORMS RM & Pricing Section prize (https://cse.umn.edu/dsi/william-l-cooper, accessed 2026-07-12).

Terminology. "Unconstraining" is the revenue-management term for estimating true demand from constrained (censored) sales data; synonyms in the literature are "detruncation", "demand uncensoring", "spill analysis", and "censored data analysis" (Guo, Xiao & Li, "Unconstraining Methods in Revenue Management Systems: Research Overview and Prospects", Advances in Operations Research, 2012, article 270910, surveys 130+ references and five families of methods: https://www.hindawi.com/journals/aor/2012/270910/, accessed 2026-07-12; Wiley mirror: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2012/270910, accessed 2026-07-12 — both endpoints returned HTTP 402 to direct fetch, so details here come from indexed abstracts; flagged as partially verified). In retail OM the same problem is called "lost sales estimation" or "demand estimation with unobservable lost sales". Azadeh, Marcotte & Savard, "A taxonomy of demand uncensoring methods in revenue management", Journal of Revenue and Pricing Management 13, 2014, pp. 440-456, DOI 10.1057/rpm.2014.8, classify methods into four categories: basic, statistical, choice-based, and optimization-based (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/rpm.2014.8, accessed 2026-07-12).

Magnitude of the bias in retail: the FreshRetailNet-50K team (Dingdong, a Chinese fresh-grocery chain) measured 7.37% systematic demand underestimation (weighted percentage error) from raw sales versus recovered demand, with hourly stockout rates following a U-shaped diurnal cycle from under 2% at 06:00 to 26% by 20:00 (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2, accessed 2026-07-12).

=== 2. MISSING vs ZERO vs IMPUTE ===

Three practical treatments of stockout periods in training data:

(a) Treat as ZERO (naive): conflates "zero sales while in stock" with "zero sales because out of stock". AWS's Amazon Forecast blog states this plainly: "The information that you sold zero units of an available item is very different from the information of selling zero units of an out-of-stock item", and "zero filling in the target time series might cause the predictor to be under-biased in its predictions" (https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/managing-missing-values-in-your-target-and-related-datasets-with-automated-imputation-support-in-amazon-forecast/, accessed 2026-07-12).

(b) Treat as MISSING (mask/NaN): removes the bias in the point forecast and widens predictive uncertainty over the censored region; Amazon Forecast supports nan/zero/value/median/mean/min/max filling with distinct strategies for middle-fill, back-fill, and future-fill, and recommends nan for out-of-stock periods so the model learns "no data exists" rather than "nothing sold" (same AWS blog URL, accessed 2026-07-12). Zalando does exactly this in training: "not only zero padded values are masked out but also all data points with zero stock availability" (see Section 5). Tradeoff: masking discards the partial information that demand was AT LEAST the observed sales (a lower bound), and for long stockouts it can leave seasonal peaks unlearned; masking also fails when stockout flags themselves are unreliable (phantom inventory, Section 4).

(c) IMPUTE uncensored demand, then forecast on the completed series: the two-stage approach. FreshRetailNet-50K demonstrates it as the paper's headline use case: stage 1 reconstructs latent demand during stockout hours from in-stock hours plus covariates (d = y⊙s + d̂⊙(1−s), where s is the hourly stock indicator), stage 2 trains the forecaster on recovered daily demand. Imputing with TimesNet cut TFT forecast WPE from -7.37% to +2.58% and WAPE from 31.75% to 29.02% (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2, accessed 2026-07-12). Tradeoff: imputation bakes model error into the training target and can overstate demand if substitution absorbed some of the lost sales (Section 4); Lokad's critique of Amazon's DIM paper makes the same point for slow movers, where "reasonable corrected demand" does not exist (https://www.lokad.com/blog/2023/12/19/deep-inventory-management-opinionated-review/, accessed 2026-07-12).

A cheap widely used variant of (c) is rate-based / seasonal-profile scaling: estimate the within-day or within-week sales profile from comparable fully in-stock periods, then scale the pre-stockout sales up by the inverse of the in-stock fraction of the profile (if you sold 10 units and were in stock for the half of the day that normally carries 50% of demand, impute ~20). Blue Yonder's public FAQ describes exactly this arithmetic for its "Unconstrained Demand" / "True Demand" concept: "what would have sold if you had infinite stock — for example, if you sold 10 units but were out of stock for half the day, the true demand was likely 20 units" (https://info.blueyonder.com/retail-planning-category-management/what-is-retail-demand-forecasting, accessed 2026-07-12). The academically rigorous version of this idea is the stockout-timing literature (Section 3, Jain/Rudi/Wang; Sachs & Minner).

=== 3. METHOD FAMILIES ===

3.1 Revenue-management lineage (airline/hotel origins).
- EM unconstraining: observed bookings for closed classes are treated as censored draws from an underlying (usually normal) distribution; E-step replaces censored observations with conditional expectations above the censoring point, M-step re-estimates mu/sigma; iterate. The CRAN RM2 package documents the canonical algorithm ("EM: Unconstrain the demand using the Expectation-Maximization", https://rdrr.io/cran/RM2/man/EM.html, accessed 2026-07-12). Talluri & van Ryzin's textbook, The Theory and Practice of Revenue Management (Springer, 2004), is the standard reference codifying EM and related estimation for RM (cited throughout the survey literature, e.g. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/rpm.2010.1, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Projection Detruncation (PD): a fixed-point variant of EM attributed to Hopperstad/Boeing in which the analyst chooses the probability tau that true demand exceeds the estimate, controlling aggressiveness; treated alongside EM in every comparison study (survey coverage: https://www.hindawi.com/journals/aor/2012/270910/, accessed 2026-07-12; flagged: the full survey text could not be opened, so the Hopperstad attribution rests on secondary citations).
- Weatherford & Pölt, "Better unconstraining of airline demand data in revenue management systems for improved forecast accuracy and greater revenues", Journal of Revenue and Pricing Management 1(3), 2002, pp. 234-254: simulation shows EM and PD are the most robust; at 60-80% censored data the sophisticated methods raise the unconstrained-mean estimate 20-80% over naive methods; on actual bookings from a major US airline (Lufthansa co-author) better unconstraining yields 2-12% revenue gains (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/palgrave.rpm.5170027, accessed 2026-07-12; PDF summary via https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233494648_Better_unconstraining_of_airline_demand_data_in_revenue_management_systems_for_improved_forecast_accuracy_and_greater_revenues, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Queenan, Ferguson, Higbie & Kapoor, "A Comparison of Unconstraining Methods to Improve Revenue Management Systems", Production and Operations Management 16(6), 2007, pp. 729-746: reviews naive methods (discarding censored observations, mean imputation), life-table, EM, PD, and proposes a double-exponential-smoothing (Holt) based unconstraining that projects the demand curve through the censored region; their method wins on 2 of 3 datasets and they show on hotel/casino booking data that skipping unconstraining causes significant revenue loss (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1937-5956.2007.tb00292.x, accessed 2026-07-12). The IJF lost-sales paper confirms Queenan et al.'s DES method as the prior ad hoc solution for lost-sales forecasting (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207023000961, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Life-table / Kaplan-Meier style: van Ryzin & McGill applied statistical life tables to unconstraining; known to produce biased estimates and only distribution-level (mean/sd) output rather than per-observation imputations (secondary coverage in the GP unconstraining paper, https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/fowkes/papers/unconstraining.pdf, accessed 2026-07-12; flagged as secondhand). Liu et al. (2002) built a parametric regression for unconstrained hotel demand from censored bookings (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233649513_Estimating_unconstrained_hotel_demand_based_on_censored_booking_data, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Small-demand regime: Kourentzes, Li & Strauss, "Unconstraining methods for revenue management systems under small demand", Journal of Revenue and Pricing Management 18(1), 2019, pp. 27-41, DOI 10.1057/s41272-017-0117-x: EM performs poorly with few sales events per product; they propose variants of intermittent/small-count forecasting methods for unconstraining (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41272-017-0117-x and open PDF https://kourentzes.com/forecasting/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Kourentzes_2017_Unconstraining.pdf, both accessed 2026-07-12; the Springer page requires login and the PDF did not parse under automated retrieval, so method details rest on the abstract and search index; flagged).
- History surveys: Weatherford, "The history of unconstraining models in revenue management", JRPM 15, 2016, pp. 222-228, DOI 10.1057/rpm.2016.10, surveys 40+ articles over ~40 years of RM unconstraining (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/rpm.2016.10, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Gaussian processes: Price, Fowkes & Hopman, "Gaussian Processes for Demand Unconstraining" (arXiv 1711.10910, 2017; journal version EJOR 275(2), 2019, pp. 621-634): GP regression with a custom non-stationary covariance imputes the censored segments of booking curves; beats single-class unconstraining baselines under nonlinear trends, long constrained periods, and changepoints (https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10910 and https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v275y2019i2p621-634.html, both accessed 2026-07-12).

3.2 Retail parametric MLE lineage (the direct ancestors of SKU-level unconstraining).
- Nahmias, "Demand estimation in lost sales inventory systems", Naval Research Logistics 41(6), 1994, pp. 739-757: IID normal demand, demand unobserved in stockout periods (you know only demand >= sales); compares MLE, BLUE, and a new estimator for mu/sigma; assumes a constant censoring level (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/1520-6750(199410)41:6%3C739::AID-NAV3220410605%3E3.0.CO;2-A, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Agrawal & Smith, "Estimating negative binomial demand for retail inventory management with unobservable lost sales", Naval Research Logistics 43(6), 1996, pp. 839-861: on data from a major retail chain, negative binomial fits SKU-level demand significantly better than Poisson or normal; they derive an NB parameter-estimation method compensating for unobserved lost sales (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/(SICI)1520-6750(199609)43:6%3C839::AID-NAV4%3E3.0.CO;2-5, accessed 2026-07-12). These two papers define the standard "censored MLE for normal / negative binomial demand" toolkit cited by nearly everything later.

3.3 Tobit / censored-likelihood time-series models.
- The Tobit model (Tobin 1958) is the canonical censored-regression framework (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobit_model, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Trapero, de Frutos & Pedregal, IJF 2024 (citation above): first application of the Tobit Kalman filter to supply-chain demand forecasting; state-space model whose observation equation is censored at the (time-varying) stock level; outperforms the Queenan-style exponential-smoothing correction, handles trends, and in a newsvendor evaluation cuts lost sales and excess stock (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207023000961, accessed 2026-07-12). Key practical point: the censoring level varies per period with on-hand inventory, unlike classical Tobit with a fixed threshold.
- Pedregal, Trapero & Holgado, "Tobit Exponential Smoothing, towards an enhanced demand planning in the presence of censored data", arXiv 2407.17920, 2024: embeds a Tobit likelihood in state-space ETS; "reduces considerably the forecast bias" on simulated and real airline and supply-chain data (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17920, accessed 2026-07-12). Follow-up with temporal aggregation: "Censored Data Forecasting: Applying Tobit Exponential Smoothing with Time Aggregation", arXiv 2409.05412 (https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05412, accessed 2026-07-12); the search snippets state Tobit models deliver cycle service levels significantly closer to targets.

3.4 Stockout-timing and survival-style methods (retail).
- Jain, Rudi & Wang, "Demand Estimation and Ordering Under Censoring: Stock-Out Timing Is (Almost) All You Need", Operations Research 63(1), 2015, pp. 134-150, DOI 10.1287/opre.2014.1326: Bayesian updating using only WHEN the stockout occurred recovers most of the information lost to censoring; an early stockout implies high demand intensity. Knowing the stockout time captures nearly all the value of full demand observability for ordering decisions (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2014.1326; INSEAD record https://www.insead.edu/faculty-research/publications/journal-articles/demand-estimation-and-ordering-under-censoring-stock; PDF https://web2-bschool.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/media_rp/publications/6isNE1485323583.pdf, all accessed 2026-07-12).
- Sachs & Minner, "The data-driven newsvendor with censored demand observations", International Journal of Production Economics 149, 2014, pp. 28-36: motivated by data from a large European retail chain; uses hourly sales-timing patterns to infer unsatisfied daily demand, then a linear-programming newsvendor with exogenous covariates (price, temperature); using timing observations eliminates ~76% of the expected-profit loss versus using only stockout-event indicators; works well under heavy censoring (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092552731300203X; RePEc: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:proeco:v:149:y:2014:i:c:p:28-36, both accessed 2026-07-12).
- Kaplan-Meier for inventory control: Huh, Levi, Rusmevichientong & Orlin, "Adaptive Data-Driven Inventory Control with Censored Demand Based on Kaplan-Meier Estimator", Operations Research 59(4), 2011, pp. 929-941: the product-limit estimator redistributes the probability mass of censored observations to uncensored ones to rebuild the demand CDF; their adaptive policy converges almost surely to optimal base-stock levels for discrete demand (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/oropre/v59y2011i4p929-941.html, accessed 2026-07-12).

3.5 Decision-theoretic results on censoring (how much censoring costs you).
- Besbes & Muharremoglu, "On Implications of Demand Censoring in the Newsvendor Problem", Management Science 59(6), 2013, pp. 1407-1424: characterizes worst-case regret with sales data versus demand data; censoring changes the exploration-exploitation tradeoff, with active exploration (deliberate over-ordering to observe demand) essential in the discrete-demand case (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v59y2013i6p1407-1424.html; SSRN https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1983270, both accessed 2026-07-12).
- Hssaine & Sinclair, "The Data-Driven Censored Newsvendor Problem", arXiv 2412.01763 (2024, v3 2026): offline setting with only censored sales; distributionally robust min-max-regret; gives a necessary and sufficient condition for vanishing regret and proves an insurmountable lower bound from censoring that persists with infinite samples (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01763, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Ding, Puterman & Bisi's classic result (censored newsvendor should order above the myopic level to buy information) sits behind this literature, verified here only through the secondary trail — flagged (related primary: Lariviere & Porteus "The Censored Newsvendor and the Optimal Acquisition of Information", Operations Research 50(3), 2002: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.50.3.517.7752, accessed 2026-07-12).

3.6 ML / deep learning with censoring.
- Censored quantile regression pattern: Ozhegov & Teterina, "Ensemble Method for Censored Demand Prediction", arXiv 1810.09166, 2018: two-part model (classifier for zero sales, regressor for positive sales) following censored quantile regression; ensembles of OLS/Ridge/Lasso/Random Forest with censorship adjustment beat unadjusted variants and recover bias-corrected price sensitivities (https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09166, accessed 2026-07-12; companion chapter: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-13709-0_37, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Feature-based newsvendor as quantile regression: Ban & Rudin, "The Big Data Newsvendor: Practical Insights from Machine Learning", Operations Research 67(1), 2019, pp. 90-108: ERM with newsvendor (pinball) loss is high-dimensional quantile regression; best one-step algorithm beats best-practice benchmark by 24% out-of-sample on hospital data (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2018.1757, accessed 2026-07-12). Not itself a censoring paper, but it is the standard scaffold onto which censored losses are bolted.
- Deep censored-loss / imputation: FreshRetailNet-50K (Section 6) benchmarks SAITS, ImputeFormer, iTransformer, GPVAE, CSDI (diffusion), TimesNet, DLinear as latent-demand-recovery models; TimesNet best (27.62% WAPE, 1.43% WPE recovery accuracy; decoupling score 0.07 vs -0.57 for raw sales) (https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16319v2, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Survival-analysis deep learning exists for right-censored outcomes (DeepQuantreg, Computational Statistics & Data Analysis 2021: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947321001572, accessed 2026-07-12), but no widely cited retail-demand paper applies deep survival losses directly on SKU sales — flagged as a thin area.
- New-product/cold-start with censoring: "Deep learning for new fashion product demand prediction: integrating visual similarity and demand correction in cold-start scenarios", International Journal of Data Science and Analytics, 2026: EM-based adjustment of censored sales records combined with ResNet-50 visual features (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41060-025-00888-8, accessed 2026-07-12). Also "Sales Forecasting for Fashion Products Considering Lost Sales", Applied Sciences 12(14):7081, 2022 (https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/14/7081, accessed 2026-07-12).

=== 4. RETAIL/CPG SUBSTITUTION AND ECONOMETRICS (what "lost" sales really are) ===

Unconstraining a single SKU in isolation overstates lost demand when customers substitute within the category, and understates category demand when they walk. The substitution-aware lineage:
- Anupindi, Dada & Gupta, "Estimation of Consumer Demand with Stock-Out Based Substitution: An Application to Vending Machine Products", Marketing Science 17(4), 1998, pp. 406-423: Poisson arrivals per product, first-choice plus one-step substitution; estimated from periodic inventory observations of an 8-product soda vending machine; separates lost demand from substituted demand (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.17.4.406, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Conlon & Mortimer, "Demand Estimation Under Incomplete Product Availability", American Economic Journal: Microeconomics 5(4), 2013, pp. 1-30 (NBER WP 14315): wireless monitoring of 54 vending machines every 4 hours; EM-corrected random-coefficients demand estimation when availability is observed only periodically; the corrected model predicts significantly larger profitability impacts of stockouts than models ignoring availability (https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fmic.5.4.1; https://www.nber.org/papers/w14315, both accessed 2026-07-12).
- Vulcano, van Ryzin & Ratliff, "Estimating Primary Demand for Substitutable Products from Sales Transaction Data", Operations Research 60(2), 2012, pp. 313-334, DOI 10.1287/opre.1110.1012: MNL choice + nonhomogeneous Poisson arrivals; observed sales treated as incomplete observations of "primary (first-choice) demand"; EM yields a simple iterative estimator whose limit points are stationary points of the incomplete-data likelihood; needs only sales, availability, and an aggregate market-share estimate (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1110.1012; SSRN https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1923711; open PDF https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/5397/vanRyzin_Estimating.pdf, all accessed 2026-07-12). This is the practical bridge from airline unconstraining to retail assortments.
- Related: Kök & Fisher-line assortment work "Demand Estimation and Assortment Optimization Under Substitution: Methodology and Application", Operations Research 55(6), 2007 (https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.1070.0409, accessed 2026-07-12).

Phantom stockouts (inventory record says in stock, shelf is empty) corrupt the availability flags every censoring method depends on:
- DeHoratius & Raman, "Inventory Record Inaccuracy: An Empirical Analysis", Management Science 54(4), 2008: 65% of ~370,000 audited records at a large retailer were inaccurate (the 65% figure is widely quoted; the direct source here gives 50-70% of SKUs affected at any time: https://ecrloss.com/research-paper/predicting-inventory-record-inaccuracy/, accessed 2026-07-12; journal page https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1070.0789, accessed 2026-07-12 — exact 65% number not re-verified from the paper text; flagged).
- Chen (2021), "Fixing Phantom Stockouts: Optimal Data-Driven Shelf Inspection Policies": data-driven inspection finds lost sales of ~3.0% versus 0.2% when trusting the inventory record (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345980410_Fixing_Phantom_Stockouts_Optimal_Data-Driven_Shelf_Inspection_Policies, accessed 2026-07-12; ResearchGate-only sourcing, flagged as thin).

=== 5. WHAT LARGE RETAILERS AND VENDORS DO ===

AMAZON (best documented):
- Availability-Corrected (AC) demand via glance views. Madeka, Torkkola, Eisenach, Luo, Foster & Kakade, "Deep Inventory Management", arXiv 2210.03137 (2022): "demand may be censored (i.e. not observed) for time periods where there was not enough inventory". They split web page "glance views" into in-stock and out-of-stock views and reconstruct demand as D~ti = D̄ti + Ci·GV̄ti, i.e., observed demand plus a conversion rate applied to out-of-stock glance views. This is a browse-signal counterfactual, feasible only for e-commerce where intent is observable during stockouts. The corrected demand feeds a differentiable simulator in which demand, prices, and lead times are exogenous to the ordering action, so rewards are differentiable in policy parameters and the policy trains by "Direct Backprop" through the simulator. Trained on 80,000 products; real-world A/B over 10,000 products for 26 weeks reduced inventory ~12% with no revenue loss (https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03137; full text via https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/abs/2210.03137, both accessed 2026-07-12). Lokad's third-party review confirms the mechanism and criticizes it for slow movers where corrected demand is ill-defined (https://www.lokad.com/blog/2023/12/19/deep-inventory-management-opinionated-review/, accessed 2026-07-12).
- Forecasting stack: Amazon's demand forecaster lineage is documented publicly (2008 textbook methods → Sparse Quantile Random Forest → 2018 MQ-RNN/CNN "A Multi-Horizon Quantile Recurrent Forecaster" (Wen et al., arXiv 1711.11053) → Dec 2020 MQTransformer), all quantile forecasters (https://www.amazon.science/latest-news/the-history-of-amazons-forecasting-algorithm, accessed 2026-07-12; MQ paper https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.11053, accessed 2026-07-12; MQTransformer https://assets.amazon.science/2a/18/f10e063e4677a312a0f06391e525/mqtransformer-context-dependent-attention-and-bregman-volatility.pdf, accessed 2026-07-12). Flag: the public history article does not itself discuss censoring; the censoring treatment is documented in the Madeka et al. paper and in AWS Forecast docs; no standalone Amazon paper covers demand uncensoring on its own.
- AWS Amazon Forecast (productized guidance): explicit missing-value handling for out-of-stock, recommending nan filling over zero to avoid under-bias (URL in Section 2, accessed 2026-07-12).

ZALANDO: Kunz, Birr, Raslan, Ma, Li, Gouttes, Koren, Naghibi, Stephan, Bulycheva, Grzeschik, Kekić, Narodovitch, Rasul, Sieber & Januschowski, "Deep Learning based Forecasting: a case study from the online fashion industry", arXiv 2305.14406 (2023): "we do not observe demand, but only sales. Demand ... materialises only when enough stock is available." They (i) impute article-level demand when only some sizes are stocked out by assuming a multinomial size distribution learned from full-availability periods, and (ii) mask training loss on all data points with zero stock availability. Scale: ~1M+ articles, 14 European markets, weekly series, 26-week horizon, transformer encoder-decoder (6+6 layers, ~53M parameters) with a monotonic demand layer enforcing downward-sloping price response; in production in versions since 2019 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14406; full text https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.14406, both accessed 2026-07-12). This is the cleanest published example of the mask-plus-partial-imputation hybrid.

DINGDONG (fresh grocery, ~898 stores): published FreshRetailNet-50K and the two-stage recover-then-forecast pipeline (Section 6).

RELEX: public RFP documentation states "Stockout sales corrections" are "applied automatically based on available master data" and the platform maintains both unconstrained forecasts ("all available demand drivers without supply-related limitations, providing a clear view of true market demand") and constrained forecasts (https://www.relexsolutions.com/relex-demand-planning-and-sensing-rfp/, accessed 2026-07-12). RELEX also publishes on phantom inventory detection via ML (https://www.relexsolutions.com/resources/phantom-inventory/, accessed 2026-07-12) and on-shelf availability (https://www.relexsolutions.com/resources/on-shelf-availability/, accessed 2026-07-12). The exact correction algorithm is not disclosed — flagged.

BLUE YONDER: FAQ pages define "Unconstrained Demand" as true demand under infinite stock with the half-day-stockout doubling example, and describe Demand Edge for Retail as SKU-store level ML forecasting with hundreds of causal factors (https://info.blueyonder.com/retail-planning-category-management/what-is-retail-demand-forecasting and https://info.blueyonder.com/supply-chain-planning/what-is-blue-yonder-demand-edge-for-retail, both accessed 2026-07-12). Algorithm details undisclosed — flagged.

o9 / TOOLSGROUP: marketing claims only. o9 cites "65% reduction in lost sales caused by out-of-stock situations" and "3-5% reduction in lost sales" ranges (https://o9solutions.com/solutions/demand-planning, accessed 2026-07-12); ToolsGroup claims "up to a 50% reduction in lost sales" for SO99+ (https://www.toolsgroup.com/product/so99/, accessed 2026-07-12). Neither documents an unconstraining method publicly — flagged as thin/marketing-grade.

Practical synthesis for the skill document: the field-consensus pipeline is (1) detect stockout periods reliably (including phantom stockouts, which corrupt the flags), (2) either mask those periods in training (safe, loses lower-bound information; the Zalando/AWS pattern) or impute latent demand using stockout timing plus within-period sales profiles (Sachs-Minner, Jain-Rudi-Wang, Blue Yonder arithmetic) or censored likelihoods (Tobit Kalman filter / Tobit ETS with a time-varying censoring level equal to on-hand stock; EM for distribution-parameter recovery), (3) account for substitution before crediting the focal SKU with all lost sales (Vulcano-van Ryzin-Ratliff EM over MNL primary demand), and (4) where browse data exists, prefer direct counterfactual demand signals (Amazon's out-of-stock glance views x conversion) over model-based imputation. Weatherford-Pölt's 2-12% revenue-gain result and FreshRetailNet's 7.37% underestimation quantify the cost of skipping the step; Besbes-Muharremoglu and Hssaine-Sinclair bound what any method can recover.
